EMIL PLS READ: Territory TAM Analysis

Internal  ·  June 2026  ·  Ayman Omar

This dashboard models the addressable revenue available to our 10-rep team. It uses fee earner headcount, practice area billing propensity, and a 50% list price discount for accounting firms versus law. The goal was a structured view of whether our territory design gives reps a comparable shot at their number. For most of the team, it does not.

Four additional reps (Lasse Jorgensen, Sabrina Foglia, Rami Rosner, John Pavia) appear in the dashboard for context and comparison only. They are not part of the core team assessment and are not included in the headline findings or the five issues below.

What We Built

  • 235 UK law firms (50+ fee earners) from The Lawyer UK 200, Legal 500 and Chambers
  • 109 European law firms across 24 countries, with per-country data residency risk scoring
  • 94 accounting firms, anchored to EY and Grant Thornton as named Laurel customers
  • 80 US law firms (Rami Rosner) and 109 US law firms (John Pavia) from AmLaw directories, included for benchmarking comparison only

Base rate: £1,000 per fee earner per year for law, £500 for accounting. Both are multiplied by a T&M factor running from 0.30x for fixed-fee work like conveyancing up to 1.50x for pure hourly billing like international arbitration. The multiplier reflects how much incentive a firm has to track and optimise billable time.

All figures use pessimistic TAM. European accounts are discounted 20 to 90% based on data residency risk, because we have no confirmed EU data hosting. What you see is what we can realistically address today, not the theoretical ceiling.

How the Equitable / Inequitable Label Works

Every rep gets a territory equity label based on how much pipeline they have relative to their quota, measured at a 20% win rate. Three rules apply:

Inequitable (too low)

Coverage below 3x at 20%, or more than 35% below the rep's own team median. The territory is too small to give a fair shot at quota.

Equitable

Coverage between 3x and 8x at 20%, and within 35% of the team median. Quota and territory are reasonably matched.

Inequitable (too high)

Coverage above 8x at 20%. The quota is understated relative to the territory. The rep has a structural advantage over peers on the same number.

The Headline Finding

7 of the 10 core team reps carry an Inequitable territory. They either have structurally insufficient pipeline to hit quota, or a quota that does not reflect the size of what they have been given. The gaps between reps on the same quota are too large to be explained by win rate or effort.

Five Issues to Work Through

1. Account allocation follows seniority, not TAM

Within our core team, Toma Kri as the newest enterprise EMEA rep has $20.5m in territory versus Mattia Cattaruzza's $37.2m on the same $1m quota. The US reps added for context show the same pattern in sharper relief: Rami (tenured) has $40.2m, John (new) has $16.2m despite 36% more accounts. The US comparison is illustrative, not a team issue to fix. The EMEA imbalance is.

2. EU data hosting has no confirmed timeline

All European pipeline is discounted 20 to 90% depending on data residency requirements, because we have no committed date for EU hosting. Agnes Aberg's patch is the most exposed: 100% of her 76 accounts are in Nordic jurisdictions, dropping her usable TAM from $13.1m to $5.2m. Jasper loses $8.4m off his headline number for the same reason. European accounts should be treated as unqualified pipeline until hosting is confirmed.

3. Jack's territory does not support his quota at accounting pricing

Jack carries a $1.4m quota with 92% of his patch in accounting firms. At 50% of the law list price, his TAM comes to $10.7m, giving him 1.5x coverage at a 20% win rate. We also have no internal conversion data from the accounting vertical beyond EY and Grant Thornton, so win rate assumptions carry more risk here than anywhere else in the model. The territory needs more law firms or the quota needs reviewing.

4. Mid-market coverage falls short across the board

All four mid-market reps sit below 2x at a 20% win rate. Claire, Mary and Patrick are between 1.4x and 1.6x. Agnes is at 1.2x, and that number relies on EU pipeline converting, which links back to issue 2. The $875k quota looks calibrated against a bigger territory than any of them actually has. Worth deciding whether to adjust the quota, expand the accounts, or both.

5. CRM data quality is affecting the model

Four confirmed duplicate accounts sit across patches: Clyde & Co (Mattia and Lasse), DLA Piper (Mattia and Sabrina), Norton Rose Fulbright (Ronald and Sabrina), and Wilsons Solicitors (Mattia and Toma). Twenty-one of Lasse's 39 accounts show zero employees in Salesforce, so headcount had to be estimated from public data. A number of global firm records also carry worldwide headcount rather than local office figures, which inflates ARR estimates for those accounts.

TAM Logic — Model Documentation

How weighted ARR is calculated for each territory

The Model

Base rate: £1,000 per fee earner per year

Weighted ARR = Fee Earners × £1,000 × T&M Multiplier

T&M Multiplier: 0.30× (fixed fee, e.g. residential conveyancing) to 1.50× (hourly, e.g. international arbitration). Applied to the base £1,000/FE rate. Source: 166 practice areas scored against UK billing market data.

First-Come-First-Serve Bias — Account Allocation Analysis

Structural inequity from territory assignment order

In both US and EMEA enterprise territories, reps who joined earlier hold materially larger TAM than those who joined later, despite carrying identical quotas. The differential is not attributable to territory size — account pools are drawn from the same markets. It reflects earlier reps selecting higher-value accounts first.
